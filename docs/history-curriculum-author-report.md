The cases are selected for different causal mechanisms, not one essay per country or every war. South Asian and African decolonization are separate entries because combining them flattened the comparison. Korea and Vietnam are comparative subsections, not a battle catalog. Suez appears as a compact test of changed power in the WWII aftermath. Latin American independence and Qing transformation are orientation cases rather than full national histories. No claim of comprehensive world-history coverage is made.

Contemporary country/capital coverage belongs to the separate geography curriculum. This batch does not attempt to update live recognition counts, current wars, capitals or contemporary alliance membership.

## Sources and evidence discipline

Each entry has specific source links in front matter and contextual body citations. Research used original treaty/legislative texts and institutional historical accounts, including National Archives, UK Parliament, UN, OAU/AU, USHMM, German Historical Museum, German Historical Institute, National Army Museum, Ghana's parliamentary archive, Bangladesh's Liberation War Museum, Indonesia's Asian–African Conference Museum, Metropolitan Museum, British Museum, UNESCO and the US Office of the Historian.

Particularly useful evidence trails:

- Treaty of Paris 1783 and Guadalupe Hidalgo 1848: territorial clauses are distinguished from consent and control on the ground.
- Secession evidence and the Thirteenth Amendment: slavery's centrality is not obscured by unspecified states' rights; nationwide abolition is distinguished from the Emancipation Proclamation's wartime scope.
- Sykes–Picot 1916, Lausanne Article 3, and the January 1923 exchange convention: proposed zones, unresolved frontier procedure, and religious rather than purely linguistic classifications are distinguishable directly in the texts.
- Indian Independence Act 1947, the Punjab boundary-map catalog description and 1971 Dacca telegram: legislation, cartographic proposals and contemporaneous atrocity reporting are different evidence types.
- Nkrumah's 5 March 1957 statement beside the Ghana Independence Act: African political agency is not replaced by the departing power's legislative account.
- OAU AHG/Res. 16(I), 1964: inherited borders are an adopted rule to reduce conflict, not proof of natural ethnic boundaries.
- NATO Article 5 and UN Charter: treaty wording is not replaced by an automatic-war metaphor or an assumption of equal institutional power.

### Access and source limitations

The primary treaty pages, most museum accounts, UN/OAU sources, and Office of the Historian pages were readable through web research. Some National Archives UK pages, the UNISPAL historical overview and the 1914–1918 Online full article returned access restrictions; their indexed summaries and catalog descriptions were available. No restriction was bypassed. The latter is retained as further reading for the peace-conference overview; the text's core treaty chronology is also supported by other institutional sources. The Punjab map is linked as an external source-reading exercise, not embedded or asserted visually inspected.

Office of the Historian's Milestones series is retired and explicitly no longer maintained. It is used for historical orientation, not current political facts. Its broad accounts occasionally simplify local agency or chronology; the drafts avoid repeating problematic details, including treating South Vietnam's government as simply appointed by Washington, assigning an incorrect month to Bosnia's independence, or treating the Soviet breakup as uniformly peaceful.

Official institutions are not perspective-free. Treaties establish what signatories stipulated, not universal legitimacy or actual implementation. British museum and parliamentary narratives are paired where useful with Ghanaian, Bangladeshi, Indonesian, UN and primary-document material. The Bangladesh museum's casualty estimates and commemorative framing are attributed rather than reproduced as uncontested totals. Political controversy is not used to create false uncertainty about established genocide.

## Chronology and factual self-checks

- Vienna Final Act: 9 June 1815, before Waterloo on 18 June; do not say the congress began after Waterloo.
- US independence recognition: 1783; Mexican cession: 1848; Civil War: 1861–65; Thirteenth Amendment ratification: 1865.
- WWI: 1914 trigger versus later global participation; US declaration against Germany in April 1917; Brest-Litovsk March 1918; western armistice 11 November 1918; five separate settlements through 1920 and Lausanne 1923.
- Greek–Turkish exchange convention: January 1923, distinct from the July peace treaty.
- WWII: European September 1939 convention does not erase Asian 1931/1937 chronology; Japanese surrender announcement and September 2 formal instrument are distinguished.
- South Asia: independence legislation, national commemorations and August 17 boundary publication are distinct; Kashmir's control-line history is not the Radcliffe provincial award.
- Ghana 1957; Kenya 1963; OAU border rule 1964; Namibia 1990; South Sudan 2011.
- Germany 1990, USSR 1991, Czechoslovakia 1993 are different kinds of transformation; Yugoslavia was not a Soviet republic or Warsaw Pact member.
- No precise disputed partition or 1971 death count asserted. No deterministic claim that Versailles caused inevitable Nazi rule. No single proven explanation of Japanese surrender asserted.
