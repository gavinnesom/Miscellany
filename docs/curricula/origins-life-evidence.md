## Geological Time Overview

File: content/physics-space/geological-time-overview.md

Stable ID: geological-time-overview:earth-history-were-one-year-approximately-would

The existing answer/rubric states only that humans occupy a tiny fraction of Earth history; it fails to answer when.

Proposed question:
If Earth's 4.54-billion-year history were compressed into a 365-day year, approximately when would Homo sapiens, dating back about 300,000 years, appear?

Proposed answer:
About 35 minutes before midnight on December 31. The calculation is (300,000 / 4.54 billion) × 365 × 24 × 60 ≈ 34.7 minutes. This refers to Homo sapiens, not the origin of the genus Homo or the beginning of recorded history.

Proposed rubric:
Require approximately the last 35 minutes of December 31, or a correct calculation giving roughly 30–40 minutes before year-end. Accept equivalent calendar phrasing. The 300,000-year age refers specifically to Homo sapiens; do not accept millions of years for this question or answers giving only “very late.”

Evidence/assumptions: Both numerical ages already appear in the body. Fixed 365-day rather than leap-year calendar. Independent calculation: 34.7312775330 minutes. Smithsonian source already in front matter supports Homo sapiens at about 300,000 years; no new chronology claim is necessary.

## Radiometric Dating

File: content/physics-space/radiometric-dating.md

Stable ID: radiometric-dating:carbon-14-useful-archaeology-but-useless-dating

Keep current question:
Why is carbon-14 useful for archaeology but useless for dating Earth itself?

Proposed answer:
Carbon-14 has a half-life of about 5,730 years. Enough remains in relatively recent once-living material to measure its decay, but over billions of years essentially all original carbon-14 disappears, far below useful detection. Earth's age therefore requires much longer-lived isotope systems and appropriate minerals or meteorites.

Proposed rubric:
Require the short half-life compared with billions of years, measurable remaining carbon-14 for relatively recent organic material, and essentially no original carbon-14 left over Earth's age. Exact recall of 5,730 years is optional if the timescale explanation is correct. Do not pass an answer that merely says multiple clocks should agree.

Evidence/assumptions: Entire explanation is already present in the existing body. No new claim or unrelated prose change is required.
