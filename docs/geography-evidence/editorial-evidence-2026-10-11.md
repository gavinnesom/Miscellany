# Geography reference: author evidence and integration notes

Prepared and checked 2026-10-11. Content-only author package; independent review, runtime tests and rendering remain integration responsibilities. This report records sources and limits, not a claim of publication approval.

## Deliverable and coverage

Eight new canonical Markdown entries under `content/history-society/`:

1. `world-geography-reference.md`: inclusion convention, navigation, spatial-learning method and comparison of landlocked states.
2. `africa-countries-capitals.md`: 54 unique baseline countries, six subregional clusters, Congo/capital comparison and 12 exception markers.
3. `europe-countries-capitals.md`: 44 entries including Holy See, Baltic/central-plain/Danube/Mediterranean walks and institutional distinctions.
4. `west-central-asia-countries-capitals.md`: 24 entries including Palestine, waterway/plateau/Caucasus/steppe structures and major status qualifications.
5. `south-east-asia-countries-capitals.md`: 24 entries across South/East/Southeast Asia, shared islands, river systems, Bangladesh geography and capital transitions.
6. `americas-countries-capitals.md`: 35 entries, Central American branching geography, Caribbean distinctions and inland river access.
7. `oceania-countries-capitals.md`: 14 entries, ocean-centered orientation, associated-state distinctions and Naoero/Kiribati/Palau corrections.
8. `sovereignty-capitals-territories.md`: selective exceptions, membership/recognition/control framework, dependencies and capital functions.

54 + 44 + 24 + 24 + 35 + 14 = **195** unique country rows. Baseline is the 193 UN members plus Holy See and Palestine. It does not claim exactly 195 universally recognized sovereign countries. Transcontinental states appear once; region assignments and deviations from M49 are explicit. No regional capital-trivia quizzes; only two conceptual quiz questions, one in the hub and one in the exceptions guide. Both have new slug-scoped IDs, version 1, active status, answer and rubric. No readiness or learning-state changes.

## Capital verification method

Read 194 individual UN Statistics Division 2025 profiles through their public `data.un.org/legacy/en/iso/xx.html` pages. The Samoa profile was inaccessible; Apia was independently confirmed by the Samoa Tourism Authority. `capital-audit-2026-10-11.csv` provides one row per baseline country with source URL, profile label, draft label and read status. Diacritics and ordinary English forms may differ without changing the place.

The profile edition is **2025**, not a claim that every profile was updated in 2026. Later official sources override stale labels. The initial `data.un.org/en/iso/XX.html` URLs often failed; the lowercase `/legacy/` versions were readable. Full Pocketbook PDF retrieval timed out; do not claim the full PDF was inspected. Country profiles themselves were inspected.

## High-priority dated evidence

### Naoero, formerly Nauru

- Primary UN page: https://www.un.org/en/about-us/member-states/naoero
- Indexed supporting excerpt: “On 26 June 2026, Nauru informed the United Nations that it had changed its name to Republic of Naoero.”
- Retrieval: official-domain search result on 2026-10-11; full-page open returned HTTP 403. Do not describe this as a successful full-page fetch.
- Independent official corroboration, directly opened: https://metadata.un.org/skosmos/thesaurus/en/page/1004349 has preferred term NAOERO, former term NAURU and a scope note dating the change to 26 June 2026.
- ADB official document page, directly opened: https://www.adb.org/documents/republic-naoero-change-name (August 2026). It describes an attached government Finance Department letter documenting the change from Republic of Nauru to Republic of Naoero.
- Current government page: https://naoero.gov.nr/about-naoero/our-country.aspx uses Naoero.
- Australian foreign ministry protocol page, directly opened and relevant lines found: https://www.dfat.gov.au/geo/heads-of-government/naoero records the name and “No official capital.” The 2025 UN profile still has Nauru/Yaren. Draft preserves the name alias and calls Yaren the government district, not a separately designated official city-capital.

### Equatorial Guinea: Ciudad de la Paz

- Official government announcement, dated **2 January 2026**: https://www.guineaecuatorialpress.com/noticias/el_presidente_de_la_republica_proclama_la_ciudad_de_la_paz_como_capital_de_la_republica_de_guinea_ecuatorial_con_la_firma_de_un_decreto_ley
- Supporting excerpt: “proclamaba la Ciudad de la Paz como capital de la República de Guinea Ecuatorial.”
- Retrieval: official-domain search returned dated title and substantial article text. Direct page returned an iframe shell, so do not claim a full direct HTML read.
- The government text identifies a signed decree-law and presidential proclamation. Draft states proclamation/designation and explicitly does **not** assert all institutions have physically moved. The 2025 UN profile’s Malabo label is preserved in the audit as an older source value.
- Independent reporting corroborates the change: https://cadenaser.com/nacional/2026/01/16/de-malabo-a-ciudad-de-la-paz-que-hay-detras-del-cambio-de-capital-de-guinea-ecuatorial-cadena-ser/ (16 January 2026). Government political/security justifications are not adopted as objective causal history.

### Indonesia: distinguish project from effective legal transfer

- Constitutional Court report **12 May 2026**: https://en.mkri.id/news/details/2026-05-12/Capital_Relocation_Depends_on_Issuance_of_Presidential_Decree
- Indonesian version: https://www.mkri.id/berita/waktu-pemindahan-ikn-bergantung-pada-pemberlakuan-keputusan-presiden-25031
- Court describes Jakarta retaining capital functions until the presidential transfer decree. No later effective decree was verified. Draft says that explicitly; it does not assert exhaustive proof that no subsequent instrument exists.
- Nusantara Capital Authority 2028 political-capital target: https://ikn.go.id/id/posts/towards-the-political-capital-2028-nca-hosts-market-sounding-for-housing-ppp-projects (7 March 2025).
- Reviewer should recheck current decree status immediately before publication if delayed beyond this reference date.

### Chagos: signed is not in force

- House of Commons Library, **8 October 2026**, directly opened: https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/cbp-10512/
- Supporting excerpt: “The treaty has not yet come into force”.
- Page states treaty signed 22 May 2025, UK ratification paused and implementing bill fell. Draft retains these distinctions and avoids an already-completed sovereignty-transfer claim. It does not reproduce unrelated political-officeholder details.

### Somaliland: avoid obsolete zero-recognition claim

- UN DPPA briefing, **15 January 2026**: https://dppa.un.org/en/speeches-and-statements/asg-khiari-warns-security-council-of-regional-risks-following-israels
- Describes Israel’s 26 December 2025 recognition and Somalia’s rejection; draft attributes the recognition, does not equate it with universal recognition or UN membership, and does not maintain an exact recognition tally.
- Independent official corroboration, Knesset **17 June 2026**: https://main.knesset.gov.il/EN/News/PressReleases/Pages/press176261.aspx

## Other source reconciliations

- **Sri Lanka:** UN profile calls Colombo capital and Kotte legislative capital. Kotte municipal government calls Kotte administrative capital: https://www.kotte.mc.gov.lk/index.php?Itemid=175&id=25&lang=en&option=com_content&view=article . Draft explains the terminology difference and retained Colombo functions.
- **Palau:** UN profile and some DFAT country-brief text use Melekeok; current DFAT protocol page uses Ngerulmud: https://www.dfat.gov.au/geo/heads-of-government/palau . Draft identifies Ngerulmud in Melekeok State, rather than hiding source variation.
- **Kiribati:** UN profile uses Bairiki. Kiribati government sources identify South Tarawa as capital and Bairiki as a government center within it: https://www.mfed.gov.ki/node/43 and https://www.melad.gov.ki/index.php?Itemid=355&id=34&option=com_quix&view=page . This is principally a geographic-scale distinction.
- **Egypt:** State Information Service overview **30 June 2026** continues to name Cairo: https://sis.gov.eg/en/egyptians-abroad-portal/our-egyptian-identity/about-egypt/ . Draft distinguishes relocation of institutions to the New Administrative Capital.
- **South Africa:** https://www.gov.za/about-sa/south-africas-provinces gives three functions/capitals. Draft avoids implying all judicial institutions sit in Bloemfontein.
- **Switzerland:** https://www.aboutswitzerland.eda.admin.ch/en/political-system (29 April 2026) calls Bern federal city/de facto capital.
- **Yemen:** https://osesgy.unmissions.org/en/node/29188 identifies Aden as temporary capital; Sana’a remains the capital in the UN profile. Draft explicitly does not claim a live control map.
- **Bolivia, Netherlands, Benin, Côte d’Ivoire, Malaysia, Eswatini:** individual UN profile footnotes distinguish constitutional/designated and functional seats; relevant URLs linked in regional notes and audit.
- **Burundi:** https://primature.gov.bi/presentation-generale/ (28 March 2026) distinguishes Gitega political and Bujumbura economic capitals.
- **Samoa:** https://www.samoa.travel/discover/the-islands-of-samoa/upolu-island/apia/ verifies Apia when UN profile unavailable.
- **Cook Islands/Niue:** https://gg.govt.nz/publications/niue-and-cook-islands and current MFAT pages establish free-association status; these are not ordinary dependent-territory exclusions. Their capitals were additionally verified by directly reading UN profiles `https://data.un.org/legacy/en/iso/ck.html` and `https://data.un.org/legacy/en/iso/nu.html`.

## Spatial and political review

Country rows give selected orientation anchors, not exhaustive neighbor lists or border adjudication. The author checked obvious topology and common confusions (two Congos, Gambia/Senegal, Lesotho/Eswatini, two parts of Malaysia, shared Borneo/New Guinea/Timor, Dominica/Dominican Republic, enclaves/exclaves). These are manually authored geographic descriptions, not a GIS computation. Independent review should sample narrow regional passages and all exception-marked rows.

The draft avoids recognition counts, live front lines, and predictions about treaty/capital completion. It attributes political positions rather than treating a single country’s policy as global agreement. Dependencies are selective and expressly non-exhaustive. No new boundary maps are drawn. The UN Geospatial July 2026 map is linked as a companion; its catalog metadata was found, but the PDF itself was not downloaded or visually inspected. No license to reproduce it is asserted. Existing repository basemaps were inspected for availability but not inserted merely as decoration.
