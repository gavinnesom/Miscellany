California/common yarrow (*Achillea millefolium*) is a spreading perennial with fernlike aromatic leaves and flat clusters of tiny flowers. It occurs naturally across California and is useful as a low-water meadow or groundcover plant; its flowers attract bees, butterflies, and other beneficial insects.

## Growing it

- **Light:** Full sun gives the densest growth and flowering, though yarrow tolerates some part shade.
- **Water:** Keep new plants reasonably moist while roots establish. Once established it is drought tolerant; inland California plantings may benefit from occasional summer water if you want them to stay green and spread.
- **Soil:** Yarrow tolerates poor soil surprisingly well. **Drainage matters more than fertility**: rich, wet conditions tend to produce tall, floppy growth.
- **pH:** Do not obsess over it. Yarrow is broadly adaptable; a roughly neutral soil is fine.
- **Habit:** It spreads by rhizomes and can form colonies. That is an advantage when you want a carpet and a nuisance where you want a tidy specimen.

## What the plant is telling you

- **Too much water / overly rich soil:** luxuriant, tall growth that flops.
- **Too little water:** foliage loses vigor, browns, or dies back; established plants survive drought better than new transplants.
- **Happy:** dense ferny foliage, outward spread, then flower stalks rising above the mat.

## For Gavin's yard

For a soft-ish, low-water California groundcover, yarrow is a strong candidate. Establish it with regular water, then reduce irrigation. More summer water generally means greener, faster-spreading growth; less water gives a tougher, more dormant-looking summer plant.
