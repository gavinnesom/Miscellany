Puncturevine (*Tribulus terrestris*) is a low-growing annual weed whose fruit breaks into hard, viciously spined burrs -- goatheads. The burr is not an incidental annoyance: it is the plant's seed-dispersal system, hitching rides on feet, paws, tires, and machinery.

A single plant can spread into a flat mat several feet across and produce hundreds to thousands of seeds in one season. The real enemy is therefore **the seed bank**, not just the plant you can currently see.

## How to remove/control it

- **Remember the lifecycle.** Puncturevine is a warm-season annual. It germinates, spreads flat, flowers, makes burrs, and dies when freezing weather arrives.
- **Get it before seed production.** UC IPM says plants can begin flowering within about **3 weeks** of germination, so small plants are worth removing early.
- **Pull or hoe seedlings.** Hand pulling and shallow hoeing/tilling can work well on seedlings and small plants; cut older plants at the taproot before or around flowering.
- **Do not rely on mowing.** Puncturevine grows too close to the ground for mowing to control it reliably.
- **Collect dropped burrs.** Sweep or rake them up; UC IPM even recommends pressing old carpet against the ground to pick up burrs.
- **Repeat for years.** Seeds can remain viable in soil for **up to about 5 years**, so control means preventing each new generation from replenishing the seed bank.
- **Mulch can help.** Around **3 inches** of light-blocking organic mulch can suppress seedlings in beds and ornamental plantings where mulch makes sense.
- **Avoid deep tilling.** Deep tilling can bury viable seed and preserve future germination problems.

## Recognition

Look for a prostrate mat radiating from a central taproot, paired leaflets, small yellow flowers, and -- most unmistakably -- the woody multi-spined fruit.
