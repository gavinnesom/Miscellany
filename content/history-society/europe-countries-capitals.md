Europe is a peninsula-rich western extension of Eurasia, not a physically detached continent. Its political map becomes easier to learn by distinguishing **Atlantic/Baltic edges, the central plain, the Alpine–Danube corridor, and the Mediterranean peninsulas**. A state’s language, EU membership, NATO membership and continental placement are separate layers.

**Big idea:** Rivers and lowlands often link capitals more strongly than national outlines suggest. Vienna, Bratislava, Budapest and Belgrade sit along the Danube system. Their proximity helps explain both historical connections and the importance of the borders separating them.

## Four spatial walks

1. **Around the Baltic:** From Denmark move east along Germany and Poland, then Lithuania–Latvia–Estonia. Across the Gulf of Finland is Finland; Sweden lies across the Baltic to the west. Norway faces the Atlantic rather than the Baltic. Russia has Baltic access near St Petersburg and through the separated Kaliningrad region.
2. **Across the central plain:** France/Benelux, Germany, Poland, Belarus and Russia form a broad west–east orientation. The plain is not uniformly flat or culturally continuous, but it lacks an ocean-sized separation. Borders here reflect political history rather than one obvious permanent physical divide.
3. **Along the Alps and Danube:** Switzerland and Austria occupy much of the Alpine corridor. Follow the Danube east through or beside Germany, Austria, Slovakia, Hungary, Croatia, Serbia, Romania and Bulgaria toward the Black Sea. A river can be a route, a border, or both.
4. **Around the Mediterranean:** Iberia, Italy and the Balkans are distinct peninsular systems. Greece’s island geography reaches toward Türkiye; Malta sits between Sicily and North Africa. The short sea crossing between continents explains why a Europe-only mental map can obscure longstanding connections.

Russia is listed here once because its capital and historical state core are west of the Urals; the country also spans northern Asia. Türkiye, Cyprus and the South Caucasus are in the [West/Central Asia reference](/entry/west-central-asia-countries-capitals). These shelving decisions do not settle identity or political affiliation.

## Capital and status notes

- **E1 — United Kingdom:** London is the UK capital. England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland are constituent parts, not four separate UN member states. The Republic of Ireland is a separate state. Great Britain is the island containing England, Scotland and Wales; it is not synonymous with the whole UK.
- **E2 — Denmark:** This row represents the UN member state. Greenland and the Faroe Islands have distinct self-governing arrangements within the Kingdom of Denmark; Greenland is geographically North American. Do not count them again as baseline sovereign states.
- **E3 — Ukraine/Russia:** Ukraine’s internationally recognized territory and Russian occupation/annexation claims must not be conflated. Kyiv and Moscow are capitals, not markers of the current front line. The UN continues to affirm Ukraine’s territorial integrity within internationally recognized borders. [UN Secretary-General, February 2026](https://www.un.org/sg/en/content/sg/statements/2026-02-24/secretary-generals-remarks-the-security-council-ukraine-delivered)
- **E4 — Netherlands:** Amsterdam is the capital; The Hague is the seat of government. [UN profile](https://data.un.org/legacy/en/iso/nl.html)
- **E5 — Switzerland:** Bern is the federal city and de facto capital; official Swiss usage makes this distinction rather than treating it as a constitutionally designated capital like any other. [Swiss government](https://www.aboutswitzerland.eda.admin.ch/en/political-system)
- **E6 — Holy See/Vatican City:** The Holy See is the UN observer state; Vatican City is the territorial state within Rome that supports its independence. The baseline has one entry, not two. [UN observers](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/non-member-states)
- **E7 — Kosovo and Transnistria:** Kosovo has substantial but non-universal recognition and is not a UN member; Serbia maintains its claim. Transnistria has a separate de facto administration within Moldova’s internationally recognized borders. These are not equivalent legal situations. See the [exceptions guide](/entry/sovereignty-capitals-territories).

## A worked distinction: city, state, union

Brussels is the capital of Belgium and hosts major EU institutions. Strasbourg also hosts European institutions. Neither fact makes Belgium or France the sovereign owner of “Europe,” nor turns the EU into an additional country in this reference. Likewise, a country may be geographically European without belonging to the EU, and EU membership does not imply participation in every European treaty arrangement.

For recall, separate three questions: **Where is it? What state is it part of? What institutions or alliances does it participate in?** Mixing them leads to errors such as putting Norway in the EU or treating the entire island of Ireland as UK territory.

The map’s smaller inland states are especially useful tests. Liechtenstein touches Switzerland and Austria; Luxembourg touches Belgium, France and Germany; Andorra touches France and Spain. “Small European country” is not a location.
