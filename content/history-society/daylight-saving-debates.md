There are actually **two separate policy questions** that often get collapsed into “stop changing the clocks”:

- permanent **daylight saving time (DST)** — later sunrise and later sunset by the clock;
- permanent **standard time** — clock noon stays closer to solar noon.

Most sleep/circadian organizations that take a position prefer **permanent standard time**, because morning light advances/stabilizes the body clock while evening light delays it. The American Academy of Sleep Medicine argues that standard time aligns better with human circadian biology.

The U.S. already tried year-round DST during the 1970s energy crisis. Congress enacted an emergency experiment beginning in **January 1974**, but dark winter mornings were unpopular and the policy was shortened; standard time returned for part of the year later in 1974.

## Current U.S. status — August 2026

This is time-sensitive. On **July 14, 2026**, the U.S. House passed H.R. 139, the **Sunshine Protection Act**, by **308–117**, proposing permanent DST. House passage did **not** by itself make permanent DST law; Senate action and presidential approval are still required.

> **Takeaway:** “Permanent time” is not one proposal. The political preference for brighter evenings conflicts with the circadian-health argument for brighter mornings.
