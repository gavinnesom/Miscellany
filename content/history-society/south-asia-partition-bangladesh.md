In 1947 British rule ended and India and Pakistan became independent. Pakistan contained two widely separated wings; its eastern wing became Bangladesh after the liberation war of 1971. The sequence is a powerful test of the idea that one shared identity can automatically define one stable state.

**Main message:** Partition does not remove diversity. It redistributes political majorities and minorities, divides institutions and networks, and creates new questions about citizenship and security.

## Independence and partition were different decisions

Decades of political organizing, mass protest, repression, constitutional negotiation and wartime change undermined British rule. The Indian National Congress sought an independent India; the Muslim League under Muhammad Ali Jinnah argued that Muslims required political safeguards and ultimately a separate Pakistan. Neither organization represented every person within the communities it claimed to speak for.

The problem was not a timeless inability of religions to coexist. It concerned who would control a postcolonial center, what powers provinces would hold, whether safeguards were credible, and how political leaders responded to escalating insecurity and violence. British institutional choices and the hurried withdrawal were part of that process, alongside decisions by South Asian leaders.

The [Indian Independence Act](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/Geo6/10-11/30) provided for two independent Dominions from 15 August 1947. Pakistan's national commemoration is 14 August and India's is 15 August; a shared legislative date and distinct national commemorations should not be forced into a false contradiction. The rapid final timetable left little space to prepare the division of administration. [Parliament's account](https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/evolutionofparliament/legislativescrutiny/parliament-and-empire/parliament-and-the-american-colonies-before-1765/the-raj-and-independence-1914-47/).

## The geography makes the difficulty visible

Punjab in the northwest and Bengal in the east were themselves partitioned. Their populations were interspersed, not arranged in two solid blocks. A district-level majority did not mean every village, neighborhood or household had the same affiliation.

Railways, irrigation works, markets and family networks crossed proposed lines. The boundary commissions had to allocate territories inside connected systems, not divide an empty page. The [National Archives' 1947 Punjab map](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/image/b87fc06c-fab5-4526-a1e9-1909a75bce84/) distinguishes the awarded boundary from a proposed Sikh line along the Chenab. Read it as evidence of competing territorial projects, not as a map of universally agreed communities.

The Radcliffe awards were made public on 17 August, after independence ceremonies. Violence and fear drove mass movement across the new borders. Death estimates vary substantially because records are incomplete and definitions differ; a single exact toll gives spurious precision. The robust facts are vast displacement, widespread massacres, abduction and sexual violence, and long-lived loss. India retained a large Muslim population: partition never produced two religiously uniform societies. [National Army Museum account](https://www.nam.ac.uk/explore/independence-and-partition-1947).

## Princely states were another problem

British India included directly administered provinces and princely states under different relationships with the imperial government. Ending British paramountcy required decisions about accession and integration; these were not all resolved by the provincial boundary commissions.

Jammu and Kashmir's disputed accession and the war of 1947–48 followed this different path. Its later control lines should not be called the Radcliffe Line. A ceasefire or line of military control also does not by itself resolve competing sovereignty claims. The [current reference conventions](/entry/sovereignty-capitals-territories) distinguish those claims from administration without pretending to adjudicate them.

## Why two wings did not remain one Pakistan

East and West Pakistan shared a Muslim-majority basis for statehood but were separated by Indian territory. Bengali language and culture, unequal influence within state institutions, and arguments over resources and representation generated a different national struggle. The Bengali Language Movement, with its pivotal events in 1952, made language a public claim about dignity and participation. [UNESCO reporting context](https://ich.unesco.org/en/state/bangladesh-BD?cp=BD&info=periodic-reporting&topic=ar-state).

In the 1970 election the Awami League won a majority in Pakistan's national assembly. Failure to transfer power was therefore not merely a dispute over local cultural preference: it was a crisis over who could govern after a national electoral victory. The Pakistani army's crackdown in March 1971 escalated into mass atrocities and war. Bengali resistance organized as the Mukti Bahini, and a Bangladeshi provisional government pursued independence. The [Liberation War Museum](https://www.liberationwarmuseumbd.org/page/history) preserves a national narrative alongside physical and documentary evidence.

The museum's numerical estimates and commemorative framing should be identified as such rather than silently treated as an undisputed statistical reconstruction. Uncertainty over totals does not erase the extensive evidence of targeted killing and civilian suffering. A contemporaneous US consular telegram, headed “Selective Genocide,” documents alarm from observers in Dacca. [Telegram, 28 March 1971](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76ve07/d125).

## A regional war inside the Cold War

Millions fled to India, creating an enormous humanitarian and political crisis. India supported the Bangladeshi struggle and intervened militarily; Pakistani forces in the east surrendered in December 1971. Regional decisions drove the outcome, even as international alignments mattered.

The Nixon administration valued Pakistan partly as a channel to China. India strengthened ties with the Soviet Union. Those alignments shaped diplomacy but do not justify reducing Bengali independence to an American–Soviet proxy contest. The [US historical overview](https://history.state.gov/milestones/1969-1976/south-asia) is particularly useful for Washington's strategic calculations and their limits.

**Takeaway:** A border is not a substitute for a legitimate political bargain. Shared religion did not guarantee equal citizenship or accepted authority, just as a new boundary in 1947 did not eliminate minority rights or interdependence.
