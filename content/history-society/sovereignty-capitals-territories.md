“How many countries are there?” looks like a counting problem, but it begins with a classification problem. “What is the capital?” can do the same. The difficulty is not usually ignorance of a city’s name; it is that institutions, legal claims and actual control do not always line up.

**Big idea:** Keep four questions separate: **membership, recognition, sovereignty claims, and effective administration**. A map can represent one of these without accurately representing all four.

**Reference date: 11 October 2026.** This guide explains important exceptions to the [195-entry baseline](/entry/world-geography-reference). It is a selective status guide, not an exhaustive inventory of every disputed border, dependency, separatist movement or maritime claim.

## Four layers of political geography

| Question | What it tells you | What it does not settle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Is it a UN member or observer state? | Its institutional position in the UN | Universal bilateral recognition or control of all claimed territory |
| Which states recognize it? | Those states’ diplomatic/legal position | Recognition by every other state |
| Who claims sovereignty? | The asserted right to govern territory | Who actually administers it today |
| Who exercises effective control? | Practical administration, sometimes military control | Lawful title, international acceptance or permanence |

The UN is not itself a state that grants bilateral recognition. Admission to membership involves the Security Council and General Assembly; recognition is an act of other states. Conversely, a member state can continue to exist during occupation, civil war, disputed government credentials or loss of effective control over part of its territory.

## Important cases outside the baseline

| Entity / territory | Location and principal center | Why the simple baseline needs a note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Taiwan | Island east of mainland China; Taipei is the seat of its government | Governed separately by the Republic of China authorities; claimed by the People’s Republic of China. Has limited formal diplomatic recognition and is not a UN member. The PRC occupies the China seat at the UN. |
| Kosovo | Inland Balkans; Pristina | Declared independence from Serbia in 2008 and is recognized by many states, including the UK and US, but not universally. Serbia maintains its sovereignty claim; Kosovo is not a UN member. |
| Somaliland | Northwestern Somali peninsula along Gulf of Aden; Hargeisa | Declared restored independence in 1991 and has its own institutions. Somalia rejects its independence. Israel recognized it in December 2025, so “recognized by no UN member” is no longer accurate. It remains outside the UN-member/observer baseline. |
| Western Sahara / Sahrawi Arab Democratic Republic | Atlantic coast south of Morocco, north/west of Mauritania; Laayoune is a major city under Moroccan administration | Western Sahara remains on the UN list of Non-Self-Governing Territories. Morocco controls most of the territory and claims sovereignty; the Polisario Front seeks independence and the SADR has recognition from some states. Territory, claimant state and effective control must not be collapsed into one label. |
| Northern Cyprus | Northern part of Cyprus; northern Nicosia | The self-declared Turkish Republic of Northern Cyprus is recognized only by Türkiye. The Republic of Cyprus is the UN member; it does not exercise effective control over the north. |
| Abkhazia and South Ossetia | Black Sea coast and South Caucasus respectively; Sukhumi and Tskhinvali | Separate de facto authorities with limited recognition. UN usage identifies these areas with Georgia; the Georgia entry does not imply that its government controls them. |
| Transnistria / Transdniestria | Mainly east of the Dniester in Moldova; Tiraspol | Separate de facto administration within Moldova’s internationally recognized borders. The OSCE settlement framework supports Moldova’s territorial integrity with special status for the region. |
| Cook Islands | South Pacific; Avarua on Rarotonga | Self-governing state in free association with New Zealand. Outside the 193-plus-2 convention, not an ordinary colony or merely a New Zealand local-government district. |
| Niue | South Pacific east of Tonga; Alofi | Self-governing state in free association with New Zealand, also outside the UN-member/observer baseline. |

This table does not imply that these nine rows have equivalent legal status. It deliberately avoids fragile counts of diplomatic recognitions, which can depend on dates, contested withdrawals and the definition of recognition.

The distinctions are grounded in the [UK’s current Taiwan position](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/taiwan), [UK statement on Kosovo](https://www.gov.uk/government/speeches/we-continue-to-call-on-kosovo-and-serbia-to-resolve-outstanding-disagreements-through-the-eu-facilitated-dialogue-uk-statement-at-the-un-security-coun), [UN briefing on Somaliland](https://dppa.un.org/en/speeches-and-statements/asg-khiari-warns-security-council-of-regional-risks-following-israels), [UN Western Sahara record](https://www.un.org/dppa/decolonization/en/nsgt/western-sahara), and the [OSCE’s Moldova framework](https://moldova.osce.org/). These sources identify institutional positions; one government’s recognition policy is not presented as universal consensus.

## “Overseas” does not identify one constitutional status

Two islands can look similar on a map and have very different relationships with a larger state.

- **Greenland and the Faroe Islands** are self-governing parts of the Danish Realm. Greenland is physically North American; the Faroes lie in the North Atlantic. Their distance from Copenhagen does not make them separate UN members. [Danish government](https://stm.dk/en/the-prime-ministers-office/the-unity-of-the-realm/)
- **Jersey, Guernsey and the Isle of Man** are Crown Dependencies, not parts of the UK and not British Overseas Territories. Bermuda, the Cayman Islands and Gibraltar belong to the different Overseas Territory category. [UK constitutional guide](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/crown-dependencies-jersey-guernsey-and-the-isle-of-man)
- **Puerto Rico, Guam, the US Virgin Islands, American Samoa and the Northern Mariana Islands** have territorial relationships with the United States; they are not the same as the fifty states. Nor are they equivalent to the sovereign **Marshall Islands, Federated States of Micronesia and Palau**, which have Compacts of Free Association and their own UN membership. [US Interior definitions](https://www.doi.gov/node/11613)
- **French Guiana, Guadeloupe, Martinique, Réunion and Mayotte** are part of France under overseas departmental/regional arrangements; French Polynesia and New Caledonia have different constitutional arrangements. “French overseas territory” used casually should not flatten these distinctions. Mayotte is administered by France but claimed by Comoros. [French statistical definition](https://www.insee.fr/fr/metadonnees/definition/c2031)
- **Aruba, Curaçao and Sint Maarten** are constituent countries within the Kingdom of the Netherlands; **Bonaire, Sint Eustatius and Saba** have a different status within the country of the Netherlands. The word “country” in a kingdom’s constitutional vocabulary is not automatically equivalent to a separate UN member. [Dutch government](https://www.government.nl/themes/government-and-democracy/caribbean-parts-of-the-kingdom/governance-of-aruba-curacao-and-st-maarten)
- **Tokelau** is not interchangeable with the Cook Islands or Niue: the latter two are self-governing in free association with New Zealand. **American Samoa** is not the independent state **Samoa**.

For these examples, the purpose is to prevent double counting and false equivalence, not to supply travel rules, citizenship advice or a complete constitutional-law account. The [UN M49 country-or-area list](https://unstats.un.org/unsd/methodology/m49/) includes territories for statistical purposes; appearance in it does not certify sovereignty.

### A dated example: Chagos

The UK and Mauritius signed an agreement concerning the Chagos Archipelago, including Diego Garcia, in May 2025. A House of Commons Library briefing updated in **October 2026** says the treaty **had not entered into force** and UK ratification was paused. Therefore this edition does not describe the signature as an already completed transfer. Sovereignty, effective administration, military-base arrangements and the rights of Chagossians are related but different questions. [Parliamentary briefing](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/cbp-10512/)

This is exactly why a reference needs an as-of date. Copying either an older “permanent British territory” description or an announcement of a “handover” without the treaty’s status can mislead.

## Capital exceptions are not all the same problem

| Pattern | Examples | Good reading habit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Designated capital differs from main government seat | Netherlands: Amsterdam/The Hague; Bolivia: Sucre/La Paz; Benin: Porto-Novo/Cotonou | Say which function each city performs. |
| Institutions deliberately distributed | South Africa; Eswatini; Malaysia | Do not force a single city to stand for every branch of government. |
| Federal/customary center rather than an ordinary formal designation | Bern in Switzerland; Yaren in Naoero | Preserve the local terminology and avoid inventing a constitutional title. |
| Project or relocation in stages | Nusantara/Jakarta; Egypt’s New Administrative Capital/Cairo | Distinguish legal designation, effective transfer and the location of particular offices. |
| Capital claim intersects with disputed sovereignty | Jerusalem and East Jerusalem | Attribute claims and recognition rather than asserting universal agreement. |
| Government displaced or divided by war | Yemen; Sudan | Separate the national capital from temporary seats and military control. |
| Different geographic scales in source labels | South Tarawa/Bairiki; Ngerulmud/Melekeok | Ask whether the label names a settlement, administrative area or wider urban region. |

The regional pages retain the details beside the relevant country rows. Where sources use different conventions, the goal is not to choose whichever label appears most often in quizzes; it is to explain why the labels differ.

## Read a boundary as an assertion with a date

A boundary map should identify whether it shows recognized international borders, administrative divisions, claims, ceasefire lines or actual control. Historical maps additionally need a date: the “Ottoman Empire,” “British Empire” or “Soviet Union” cannot be represented honestly by one timeless outline.

An attractive hand-drawn shape is not evidence. Use a published map with provenance and a legend; preserve dashed lines and disclaimers rather than converting them to ordinary solid borders. This reference links to existing cartographic sources instead of inventing a new boundary dataset.

**Takeaway:** The exceptions are part of geographic literacy. They show why statehood, territory, administrative power and institutional membership need different words.
