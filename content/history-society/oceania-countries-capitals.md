An ordinary world map often makes Oceania look like scattered leftovers at the edge. Reverse that perspective: **the Pacific is the connecting space**, and island chains, voyages, reefs, ports and maritime jurisdictions organize the region. Land area alone gives a misleading impression of scale.

**Big idea:** An island, an archipelago, a cultural region and a sovereign state are different units. “Micronesia” can mean a broad island region or the specific Federated States of Micronesia. Polynesia includes both independent states and territories attached to states elsewhere.

## Orient around four groupings

- **Australia and New Zealand:** Australia is a continental landmass, while New Zealand is a separate island system southeast of it. Wellington sits near the strait between New Zealand’s main islands, not at the country’s northern end.
- **Melanesia:** Follow New Guinea southeast through Solomon Islands and Vanuatu toward Fiji. Western New Guinea belongs to Indonesia, whose baseline entry appears in Asia. New Caledonia is geographically in this region but is not an independent UN member state.
- **Micronesia:** Palau is east of the Philippines; the Federated States of Micronesia stretch eastward across the Caroline Islands; Marshall Islands lie farther east/northeast. Naoero and the Gilbert Islands of Kiribati lie near the equator. These are vast maritime spaces between relatively small land areas.
- **Polynesia:** Samoa, Tonga and Tuvalu are the three baseline entries grouped here. The much wider Polynesian region also includes places such as Hawaiʻi, French Polynesia, the Cook Islands, Niue and Rapa Nui, whose state relationships differ.

Kiribati spans island groups associated with both Micronesia and Polynesia; it is shelved once under Micronesia for its Gilbert Islands capital. These inherited regional names are useful orientation tools, not a claim that all island societies within a label have one history, language or political interest. New Zealand’s Māori history also belongs to Polynesian history even though this table shelves New Zealand beside Australia.

## Capital and status notes

- **O1 — Naoero:** The UN records that **Nauru notified it on 26 June 2026 of the change to Republic of Naoero**. “Nauru” remains a useful former-name search term, not an additional state. Yaren is the government district commonly used as the capital reference; the island has no separately designated official capital city. The UN’s 2025 profile still uses Nauru/Yaren. Australia’s foreign ministry explicitly records no official capital. [UN name-change notice](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/member-states/naoero), [UN thesaurus effective-date record](https://metadata.un.org/skosmos/thesaurus/en/page/1004349), [current government site](https://naoero.gov.nr/about-naoero/our-country.aspx), [Australian government](https://www.dfat.gov.au/geo/heads-of-government/naoero)
- **O2 — Kiribati:** South Tarawa is the broader capital/urban area; the UN profile lists Bairiki, an islet and government center within South Tarawa. These labels refer to different geographic scales, not rival national capitals. Institutions are distributed along the atoll’s southern islets. [UN profile](https://data.un.org/legacy/en/iso/ki.html), [Kiribati Lands Ministry](https://www.melad.gov.ki/index.php?Itemid=355&id=34&option=com_quix&view=page), [Kiribati Finance Ministry capital reference](https://www.mfed.gov.ki/node/43)
- **O3 — Palau:** Ngerulmud is the capital settlement in Melekeok State on Babeldaob. Some references, including the UN profile, use Melekeok for the capital area. Australia’s current protocol list specifies Ngerulmud; neither label means the former capital Koror. [Australian government](https://www.dfat.gov.au/geo/heads-of-government/palau)
- **Samoa source:** Apia is confirmed by the [Samoa Tourism Authority](https://www.samoa.travel/discover/the-islands-of-samoa/upolu-island/apia/); its UN profile was unavailable to the research tool.
- **Cook Islands and Niue:** These self-governing states in free association with New Zealand fall outside the 193-plus-2 baseline because they are neither UN members nor the two observer states. Their exclusion is not a claim that they are ordinary dependent territories. Their capitals are Avarua and Alofi. See the [status guide](/entry/sovereignty-capitals-territories).
- **Freely associated Pacific UN members:** Palau, the Federated States of Micronesia and Marshall Islands are sovereign UN members with compacts of free association with the United States. A compact is not the same status as a US territory such as Guam or American Samoa.

## Worked example: why the map edge is deceptive

Kiribati includes the Gilbert, Phoenix and Line island groups, spread across a large span of the central Pacific. On a Greenwich-centered map, some islands may appear toward opposite edges. That apparent discontinuity belongs partly to the projection and chosen map cut, not to an ocean barrier at the edge of the page.

Move to a Pacific-centered map and ask which islands lie west or east of one another. Then distinguish distance from calendar time: the International Date Line bends around political jurisdictions. It is not a wall in the ocean, nor is every island’s civil date determined mechanically by the 180th meridian.

This perspective also changes the meaning of “small country.” A state may have a tiny terrestrial footprint but communities separated by long sea journeys and substantial maritime interests. Exclusive economic zones are not sovereign land, and overlapping maritime claims require separate treatment; an ocean-facing reference should not color them as though they were ordinary national territory.
