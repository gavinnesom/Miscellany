A car suspension has two jobs that fight each other: **keep the tires in useful contact with the road** while **isolating the body from bumps and wheel motion**.

The spring and damper do different physics:

- A **spring** stores and returns energy. For an ideal linear spring, \(F=-kx\).
- A **damper/shock absorber** dissipates motion energy as heat; a simplified model has damping force roughly proportional to suspension velocity, \(F\approx-cv\).

Without damping, a sprung car would continue oscillating after a bump. Without springs, road impacts would be transmitted brutally into the body.

The wheel, tire, brake, hub, and some suspension parts form **unsprung mass**. Lower unsprung mass generally helps the tire follow rapid road changes because less mass has to accelerate vertically.

Geometry also matters. Control arms, struts, links, and bushings determine how **camber, toe, caster, and track** change as the wheel moves. This is why “suspension” is not merely springs and shocks.

> **Big idea:** Good suspension is controlled relative motion: let the wheel move rapidly enough to follow the road while keeping the body comparatively calm.

An **anti-roll bar** links left and right suspension movement and resists body roll in corners, but stronger roll stiffness can also change grip balance between the axles.
