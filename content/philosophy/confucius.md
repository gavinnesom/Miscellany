Confucius (traditional dates **551–479 BCE**) is central to a Chinese ethical and political tradition focused less on abstract rules than on **cultivating character through relationships, ritual practice, learning, and exemplary conduct**.

> **Big idea:** Social order is built from trained habits of humane relationship, not merely imposed by laws and punishments.

Several terms are worth recognizing:

- **ren (仁)** — humaneness/benevolence, a cultivated concern for others;
- **li (禮)** — ritual/propriety, extending from ceremonies to everyday patterns of respectful conduct;
- **junzi (君子)** — the exemplary or cultivated person, whose character makes good conduct reliable;
- **xiao (孝)** — filial respect, part of the broader idea that ethical life begins within relationships.

Confucian ritual is easy to misunderstand as empty etiquette. The deeper idea is that repeated social forms can **train emotion, attention, and behavior**, much as practicing a skill changes what becomes natural.

Politically, Confucian thought places unusual weight on moral example: rulers who govern through virtue can shape norms without relying entirely on coercion. Later Confucian traditions became diverse and sometimes very different from what can safely be attributed to the historical Confucius.

The *Analects* is a compilation produced by followers across time, not a book personally authored by Confucius in modern form.
