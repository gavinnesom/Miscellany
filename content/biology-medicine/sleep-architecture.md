A normal night's sleep is not eight hours of one state. The brain repeatedly cycles through **N1 -> N2 -> N3 -> back toward lighter NREM -> REM**, with the mixture changing over the night.

A first adult sleep cycle is often roughly **70-100 minutes**, with later cycles commonly around **90-120 minutes**, giving about **4-5 cycles** in a typical full night.

## Rough adult proportions

These are useful anchors rather than personal targets:

| Stage | Approx. share | What distinguishes it |
|---|---:|---|
| **N1** | ~5% | transition into sleep; very light |
| **N2** | ~50% | dominant stage; sleep spindles and K-complexes |
| **N3** | ~15-25% | deep / slow-wave sleep |
| **REM** | ~20-25% | rapid eye movements, wake-like cortical activity, muscle atonia |

NREM sleep as a whole is therefore roughly **75-80%** of total sleep, with REM making up most of the rest.

## The night is asymmetric

Early cycles contain more **N3 deep sleep**. As morning approaches, N3 shrinks and **REM periods become longer**. This means losing the last few hours of sleep disproportionately removes REM-rich sleep rather than simply removing an interchangeable block.

## Two clocks determine whether you sleep

- **Homeostatic sleep pressure** builds the longer you stay awake and dissipates during sleep.
- **Circadian timing** changes sleepiness/alertness according to biological time.

The interaction explains why being awake for many hours does not guarantee effortless sleep at every clock time.
