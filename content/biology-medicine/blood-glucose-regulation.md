Blood glucose homeostasis is a control system centered on the pancreas and liver, with muscle and fat acting as major fuel users/stores.

## After a carbohydrate-containing meal

- Blood glucose rises.
- Pancreatic **beta cells** increase insulin secretion.
- Insulin promotes glucose uptake in skeletal muscle and adipose tissue, promotes glycogen synthesis, and suppresses liver glucose output.
- Blood glucose falls back toward its regulated range.

## During fasting

- Dietary glucose disappears and insulin falls.
- Pancreatic **alpha cells** increase glucagon signaling.
- The liver releases glucose first through **glycogenolysis** and increasingly through **gluconeogenesis**.
- Falling insulin also permits more lipolysis and fat use.

The liver is therefore a glucose buffer: after meals it stores incoming carbon; between meals it puts glucose back into circulation.

## Useful numerical anchors

These are **diagnostic fasting-plasma-glucose cutoffs**, not a claim that healthy glucose never moves outside them:

| Fasting plasma glucose | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| **<=99 mg/dL** | normal diagnostic range |
| **100-125 mg/dL** | prediabetes range |
| **>=126 mg/dL** | diabetes range; normally requires confirmation |

For people with diabetes, glucose below **70 mg/dL** is commonly treated as hypoglycemia.
