Mitosis and meiosis both start with copied chromosomes and both use chromosome-separation machinery. Their different jobs come from **what pairs up and what gets separated**.

| | Mitosis | Meiosis |
|---|---|---|
| Main job | growth, repair, cell replacement | make gametes |
| DNA replication | once | once |
| Cell divisions | one | two |
| Human starting cell | diploid (46 chromosomes) | diploid (46) |
| Products | 2 broadly genetically similar diploid cells | 4 genetically distinct haploid cells (23) |
| Homologs pair? | no | yes, in meiosis I |
| Crossing over? | normally no | yes, between homologs |

## The conceptual trick

- **Mitosis separates replicated copies.** After DNA replication, sister chromatids are separated so each daughter cell receives a full genome.
- **Meiosis I separates homologous chromosome pairs; meiosis II then separates sister chromatids.** Maternal and paternal homologs pair in meiosis I, crossing over exchanges DNA between them, homologs separate, and the second division separates the copied chromatids.

That two-step design both halves chromosome number and reshuffles inheritance.
