DNA and RNA are chemically close relatives used for very different jobs.

| | DNA | RNA |
|---|---|---|
| Sugar | deoxyribose | ribose |
| Base | thymine (T) | uracil (U) |
| Usual form | double-stranded | often single-stranded |
| Typical role | durable information archive | message, regulator, structure, catalyst |

The small chemical difference matters. RNA's ribose has an extra **2-prime-OH group**. That hydroxyl can participate in cleavage of the phosphodiester backbone, making RNA more prone to hydrolysis than DNA. Removing that oxygen in deoxyribose helps make DNA better suited to long-term information storage.

RNA is not merely "a disposable DNA copy." Messenger RNA carries coding information, but ribosomal RNA forms catalytic/structural machinery, transfer RNA delivers amino acids, and many other RNAs regulate gene expression.

A useful analogy is therefore:

**DNA = archive; RNA = working molecular ecosystem built from selected information in the archive.**
