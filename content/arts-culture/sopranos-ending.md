The final scene of *The Sopranos* is built to make ordinary family dinner feel like a threat-detection exercise. Tony arrives at Holsten's, chooses Journey's **“Don't Stop Believin'”**, and repeatedly looks toward the door as the bell rings. Meadow struggles to park outside. Suspicious-looking strangers enter. Then, at the moment Tony looks up toward another arrival, the image and sound cut abruptly to black.

> **Main interpretation:** The blackout is strongly compatible with Tony being killed at that instant — but the episode deliberately refuses to show the event, so “the show explicitly confirms Tony's murder” goes beyond what is on screen.

The death reading is supported by the show's own setup. Earlier, Bobby Bacala says that in a killing “you probably don't even hear it when it happens,” a line the final season later recalls. The Holsten's editing also repeatedly establishes a pattern: bell → Tony looks up → we see what Tony sees. On the final bell, the expected next POV is replaced by black.

But the scene works even without treating the black screen as a literal first-person death shot. Tony's life has become permanent anticipation: every person entering the room can be a threat, indictment, betrayal, or nothing at all. The audience is forced to inhabit that uncertainty.

David Chase later discussed an **earlier conceived death-scene idea** involving Tony driving toward New York. That is evidence that death was part of his thinking, not a clean authorial footnote saying “the diner cut definitively means X.”
