Terry Pratchett's **Discworld** is a 41-novel comic-fantasy world published from **1983 to 2015**. It began close to parodying fantasy conventions and grew into a flexible machine for satire about policing, religion, racism, newspapers, banking, industrialization, education, death, stories, technology, and what people do inside institutions.

There is no single required reading order. The books share a world but often form character-centered sequences:

- **City Watch** — Sam Vimes and Ankh-Morpork policing; law, class, prejudice, institutions.
- **Witches** — Granny Weatherwax and company; stories, power, community, practical morality.
- **Death** — personified Death and family; mortality, humanity, belief.
- **Rincewind/Wizards** — strongest connection to the early fantasy-parody books.
- **Moist von Lipwig** — post office, banking, railways: institutional modernization as con-art rehabilitation.
- **Tiffany Aching** — coming-of-age witch sequence, also written for younger readers but not intellectually minor.

> **Big idea:** Discworld works because the fantasy is rarely the actual target. Trolls, dwarfs, witches, and magic create enough distance to look freshly at very human systems.

The official Pratchett sites explicitly treat the **41 novels** as readable in publication order or along character/subseries paths. That makes a future interactive Discworld map an especially good Miscellany visual.
