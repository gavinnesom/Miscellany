About **66 million years ago**, the Cretaceous ended in a mass extinction that eliminated all **non-avian dinosaurs** along with ammonites and many marine plankton groups. Roughly **three-quarters of species** are estimated to have disappeared.

The strongest smoking gun is the **Chicxulub asteroid impact** beneath today's Yucatán Peninsula. Evidence includes a global iridium-rich boundary layer, shocked minerals, impact ejecta, and a crater about **180 km across**.

The NASA/JPL image above is not fantasy impact art. It is a computer-enhanced shaded-relief/topographic view of the modern Yucatán Peninsula. The subtle semicircular trough marks the crater's outer boundary; the crater itself is mostly buried under later limestone, and the height/color exaggeration makes a very low-relief feature visible.

The killer was not simply the blast near Mexico. Dust, sulfate aerosols, soot, and ejecta spread globally, sharply reducing sunlight and photosynthesis. The resulting **impact winter** collapsed food webs on land and in the oceans.

Large eruptions in India's **Deccan Traps** were also occurring around this time and affected climate. Their exact contribution to the extinction remains debated, but the asteroid is the clearest abrupt trigger.

One useful correction: dinosaurs did **not** vanish completely. Birds are living dinosaurs.
