The **Permian–Triassic extinction**, about **251.9 million years ago**, was the most severe known mass extinction. Exact percentages depend on how the fossil record is analyzed, but estimates commonly put marine species loss above **80%**, with catastrophic disruption on land as well.

The leading culprit is not one impact but an enormous volcanic episode: the **Siberian Traps**. Vast eruptions released greenhouse gases and drove rapid warming. Cascading effects included ocean deoxygenation, acidification, habitat loss, and major disruption of the carbon cycle.

> **Big idea:** Mass extinctions need not have a single dramatic “bullet.” The Great Dying looks more like a planetary systems failure, with volcanism pushing climate and ocean chemistry across multiple biological limits.

Recovery was slow. Ecosystems remained simplified for millions of years before complex communities were re-established.
