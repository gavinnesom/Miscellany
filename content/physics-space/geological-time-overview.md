Earth history is **4.54 billion years** long, but the part most familiar from fossils occupies only a thin recent slice. The **Phanerozoic Eon** — abundant visible animal life — begins only **538.8 million years ago**, roughly the last **12%** of Earth's history.

Useful anchors:

- **4.54 Ga:** Earth forms.
- **≥3.5 Ga:** unambiguous evidence of very early life exists by roughly this time; claims of older life remain debated.
- **~2.4 Ga:** Great Oxidation Event permanently changes atmospheric/surface chemistry.
- **635–539 Ma:** Ediacaran Period; macroscopic multicellular organisms become prominent.
- **538.8 Ma:** Cambrian begins; the Cambrian diversification is a geologically short interval, not a single instant.
- **251.9 Ma:** Permian–Triassic extinction, the Great Dying.
- **late Paleozoic to ~200 Ma:** Pangaea assembles, persists, and begins breaking apart.
- **~230 Ma:** earliest dinosaurs.
- **66 Ma:** end-Cretaceous extinction.
- **~300,000 years ago:** *Homo sapiens*.

For scale, if Earth's history were compressed into **one calendar year**, *Homo sapiens* would arrive only about **35 minutes before midnight on December 31**.

> **Takeaway:** Human history is not merely short compared with geology; it is almost invisible on the planetary timescale.
