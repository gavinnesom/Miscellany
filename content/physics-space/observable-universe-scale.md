The universe is about **13.8 billion years old**, but the radius of the observable universe today is roughly **46 billion light-years**, giving a diameter near **93 billion light-years**.

That is not a contradiction. The light from the most distant observable matter has been traveling for almost the age of the universe, but **space expanded while the light was in transit**. The matter that emitted that ancient light is now much farther away than “distance = light speed × travel time” would suggest in a static universe.

The oldest electromagnetic light we directly observe is the **cosmic microwave background (CMB)**, released when the universe became transparent about **380,000 years after the Big Bang**. We infer earlier epochs indirectly.

> **Big idea:** The observable universe is a horizon, not an edge. It marks what could have sent us information since the early universe; the whole universe may extend vastly farther and may even be spatially infinite.

A scale anchor: our Milky Way is about **100,000 light-years** across. The observable universe is therefore close to a **million Milky-Way diameters** across.
